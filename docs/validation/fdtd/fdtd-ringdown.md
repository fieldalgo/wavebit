TL;DR: `fit_fdtd_modes` extracts damped exponentials from a point trace, with
explicit model order and residuals. It recovers known Maxwell/material modes
and source-free records generated by Meep. Applying it to the remaining
near-Rayleigh tail does **not** reveal a trustworthy single Q: the dominant
frequency follows the discrete cutoff, while the fitted decay changes with
the time window. An algebraic envelope describes this record better.

## Method and scope

The [guide](../../guide/fdtd/time-domain.md#fitting-frequencies-and-decay-rates-from-a-time-trace)
defines units, options and output. The implementation forms two shifted Hankel
matrices, truncates the singular-value decomposition to the requested rank,
and solves the resulting small eigenproblem. Mode amplitudes are then obtained
by linear least squares. This is an independent implementation of the
matrix-pencil construction in [Hua and Sarkar (1990), section II](https://doi.org/10.1109/29.56027).
No external inversion implementation is used. Signal normalization and bounded
exponential columns handle very small/large signals and growing modes.

This is offline NumPy analysis. It neither changes the time loop nor replaces
the existing convergence diagnostics. Its residual describes reconstruction of
the chosen samples, not uncertainty in frequencies or physical boundaries.
The [public Meep guide](https://meep.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Introduction/)
also distinguishes time-domain mode extraction from field computation and
describes missed/spurious-mode limitations. Meep/Harminv implementation code
was not consulted or incorporated.

## Independent checks

Synthetic sums check complex frequency signs, damping and growth, amplitudes at
the selected time origin, real conjugate pairs, and signal scales 1e-240 through
1e240. Noisy frequencies 1.000 and 1.007 are recovered over a 49.9-unit record
with absolute frequency/decay tolerance 2e-5. An underfit reports a larger
residual; zero, malformed, nonuniform and rank-deficient records are refused.
An explicit alias test shows that an undersampled frequency is reported on its
sampled branch, not recovered by inference.

Uniform native Lorentz records are checked on NumPy, JAX and Torch against an
independently constructed discrete oscillator recurrence. Constant displacement
gives a DC term plus conjugate oscillations at a continuous rate satisfying
`omega^2 = omega0^2 + strength/epsilon`. Time-step refinement from 0.08 to 0.04
to 0.02 approaches the continuous damped-oscillator frequency at second order.

Two additional source-free traces were generated by Meep 1.34.0 on CPU, using
only its documented public simulation API. Their sampled values are frozen;
ordinary tests do not import or install Meep. Each fit is compared against the
independent **discrete** Maxwell/material characteristic polynomial, with
absolute frequency and decay tolerance 1e-9.

| External trace | Positive fitted frequency | Amplitude decay rate | Q | Relative residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Uniform Lorentz medium | 0.503565717 | 0.0500000667 | 31.639925 | 9.82e-14 |
| Conductive periodic cavity mode | 0.704387651 | 0.0500000167 | 44.257967 | 5.09e-14 |

These are fitted Meep **field records**, not a comparison with Meep's Harminv
frequency estimates. The uniform material uses epsilon=2, omega0=3, gamma=0.1
and strength=2, with dt=0.04. Its trace contains the DC term and needs three
exponentials. The periodic cavity uses epsilon=2, normalized electric damping
0.1, a spatial cosine of period 1, dz=0.05 and dt=0.02, and needs two modes.
Both record every 0.08 units for 40 units after the source ends. These controls
do not validate an open grating's lifetime or arbitrary multimode noisy data.

## Remaining near-Rayleigh signal

The study retains the [CPML-strength](fdtd-pml-strength.md) binary grating,
resolution 40, pulse width 60, 800 periods, physical PML length 0.6 and nominal
target 1e-40. It records native Ey at (x,y,z)=(-0.2,0,0.1) and Ez at
(-0.2,0,0.1125), with equal coherent s/p drive, every 10 updates. Both components
are electric fields and use their own native coordinates. The last sample is
at approximately 768.00. Fits use 128 Hankel columns.

The two boundary-clearance settings leave the structure and near extraction
planes unchanged. Padding scale 1 places the PML inner faces at z=-2.0/2.2;
scale 2 places them at -3.9/4.1. The source position also follows scene padding,
so this is not a change to PML distance alone. Here the relevant transverse
wavevector is `kx=2*pi*(0.2/0.96-1/0.8)`. Its Yee lower-cutoff frequency is

```text
f_cut = asin(dt * abs(sin(kx*dx/2))/dx) / (pi*dt)
      = 1.040752260658,
```

versus the physical threshold 1.041666666667. This calculation is independent
of the fitting algorithm. The fitted frequency below is close to the cutoff,
not the earlier approximately 1.01807 TM oscillation suppressed by source
narrowing.

| Padding | Ey fit start | Single-exponential frequency | Decay rate | Relative residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 540 | 1.040731875 | 0.00521754 | 0.03306 |
| 1 | 600 | 1.040742705 | 0.00441620 | 0.01250 |
| 1 | 660 | 1.040751286 | 0.00395143 | 0.003881 |
| 2 | 540 | 1.040731401 | 0.00520579 | 0.03495 |
| 2 | 600 | 1.040741377 | 0.00435847 | 0.01355 |
| 2 | 660 | 1.040748034 | 0.00385317 | 0.004354 |

The decay rate moves with the fitting window in both padding cases. A
descriptive power law uses the known Gaussian peak t=300:
`A*((t-300)/(t_start-300))^(-p)*exp(-2j*pi*f*(t-t_start))`.
For Ey, padding 1 gives p=1.6022 over t>=600 and 1.6077 over t>=660, with
residuals 0.001518 and 0.000751. Padding 2 gives p=1.5757/1.5654 and residuals
0.001489/0.000516. Ez follows the same pattern; at padding 2, its exponents are
1.5627/1.5536 and residuals 0.001121/0.000472. The fitted exponent is not fixed
to a theoretical value, and these finite windows do not establish a universal
asymptotic exponent.

Adding exponentials reduces the fitting residual without making each one a
physical resonance. At padding 1, Ey and t>=660, a two-mode fit gives residual
2.70e-5, while three modes give 6.23e-7 and an apparent growing component
(decay=-0.00526) of small amplitude. Mode count and window changes move these
parameters. This is not evidence of instability in the passive simulation.

Together, the cutoff-frequency match, window-dependent exponential decay,
better algebraic fit and persistence under increased clearance support a
cutoff-related, nonexponential tail as the current explanation. They do not
prove the absence of boundary error or a complete modal expansion of the open
system. A sum of fitted exponentials must not be used here to declare Q or
silently correct the infinite-time DFT. Long-window and spatial/boundary
convergence remain necessary.

## Reproduce

```shell
python -m studies.fdtd.ringdown.fdtd_ringdown --pad-scale 1 --save-records artifacts/ringdown-1.npz
python -m studies.fdtd.ringdown.fdtd_ringdown --pad-scale 2 --save-records artifacts/ringdown-2.npz
python -m studies.fdtd.ringdown.fdtd_ringdown --load-records artifacts/ringdown-1.npz
```

The default backend is double-precision JAX; `--backend numpy` uses the same
time-domain engine. The archive allows new window/model comparisons without
rerunning the grid. The independent external-record generator and its conditions
are described in `tests/references/README.md` in the checkout.
